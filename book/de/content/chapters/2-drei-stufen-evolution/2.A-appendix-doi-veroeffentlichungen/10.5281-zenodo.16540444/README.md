## Abstract

This paper presents a three‑step conceptual evolution of Martian habitat construction,
progressing from traditional engineering principles to biomimetic lightweight materials
and ultimately to autonomous, self‑growing silicon‑based structures. Building upon the
foundational Mars Rubber‑Glass House Project, this work outlines a scalable, sustainable
pathway for future Mars habitats.

Each phase is grounded in current or emerging material science: from titanium‑silicone‑glass
hybrid domes to silica‑aerogel frameworks with biological reinforcement patterns,
culminating in synthetic structures capable of growth, self‑repair, and adaptation using
Martian resources. The paper discusses technical principles, material candidates,
environmental integration, and the long‑term vision of autonomous life‑supporting
architecture for space colonisation.
