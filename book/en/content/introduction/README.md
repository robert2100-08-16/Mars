# Introduction

## Why Mars?

Human settlement of Mars is no longer a question of *if* but of *when*. With
launch costs falling rapidly — SpaceX's Starship targets below \$200/kg to low
Earth orbit — the economic barrier to interplanetary construction is eroding
faster than most roadmaps predicted.

Yet the engineering challenges remain formidable. Mars offers a surface gravity
of 0.38 g, an atmosphere of 95 % CO₂ at less than 1 % of Earth's sea-level
pressure, temperatures swinging between −60 °C and +20 °C, and a radiation
environment unshielded by any significant magnetosphere.

## Scope of This Book

This volume collects the author's work across three complementary pillars of
Mars surface operations:

### Part I — Mars Habitats

The centrepiece is the *Rubber-Glass House* concept: a large-scale inflatable
habitat whose transparent ETFE membrane admits enough photosynthetically
active radiation (PAR) to sustain indoor agriculture while a layered
silica-aerogel insulation system maintains habitable temperatures with minimal
energy input. Five chapters trace the concept from first design through
variants, cost estimation and a community-oriented "People's House" proposal
to concrete v2 design improvements informed by habitability engineering.

### Part II — Biomimetic and Self-Growing Structures

A single visionary paper sketches a three-step evolution: from classical
inflatable construction, through biomimetic silica-aerogel composites inspired
by diatoms and radiolaria, to a speculative stage in which habitats grow
autonomously from regolith feedstock using programmed silicon-based polymers.

### Part III — Mars Robotics

Before humans set foot on the surface, autonomous systems must survey terrain,
deploy infrastructure and maintain equipment. The *Mars Field Laboratories*
chapter proposes teams of legged robots and cooperative swarm clusters that
operate as mobile science platforms.

## How to Read This Book

Each chapter is self-contained and was written — or can be read — as an
independent paper. Cross-references link related concepts across chapters.
Published papers carry their Zenodo DOI in the chapter header for citation
purposes.

The appendices include the full license text and a colophon documenting the
toolchain used to produce this book.
